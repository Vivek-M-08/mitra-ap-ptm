# Metric 3: Challenges That Require Attention

The following themes emerge from an aggregated analysis of parent complaints rated 1 and 2 across Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam schools throughout Andhra Pradesh. Each challenge is presented with its scope, evidence, and recommended corrective action.

---

**Challenge Title:** Drinking Water Access and Quality Failures

**Description:**
Drinking water is the single most prevalent complaint category, dominating low-rated feedback across nearly every district. Parents report that children lack access to clean, purified, or mineral water on a daily basis. Specific grievances include: "No water fasetelus," "Mineral Water Not Provided," "Worst drinking water was not purified," "Water problem," "Drink ing Water not available surrounding places," and "Because boar water." In several schools, parents note that children are forced to drink unfiltered or bore water, and that RO plants are either absent, non-functional, or produce water only once a day. The emotional urgency of the complaints — including a parent noting "Children are only getting filtered water once a day 😥" — signals a systemic failure in providing a basic necessity to residential students.

**Challenge Title:** Inadequate Food Quality and Diet Menu Deviations

**Description:**
Parents across multiple districts report persistent failures in the quality, taste, quantity, and adherence to the prescribed diet menu. Complaints range from general dissatisfaction — "Given only first aid treatment only," "Very disappoint," "assalu menu prakaram pettaru" — to serious food safety concerns: "sometimes in rice they see some insects and some other kind of problems facing," "They are serving uncooked rice," and "food is good" (noted sarcastically as the sole positive in an otherwise heavily negative dataset). High-volume schools like Eluru's Dwarka Tirumala campus generated complaints across multiple categories simultaneously, with one parent noting "There is no proper cooking." First-year versus second-year quality degradation was specifically called out: "For first year food was excellent but in second year it's very poor." These complaints suggest inconsistent kitchen oversight and potential neglect in food preparation standards.

**Challenge Title:** Caretaker Absence, Neglect, and Inappropriate Conduct

**Description:**
The role of the caretaker — critical in residential settings where children live away from parents — is a recurring source of deep concern. Parents report a spectrum of failures: complete absence of a caretaker ("There is no caretaker," "No care ticker"), verbal abuse ("caretaker was not good he always scolds students the way he talks was rash and rude"), and neglect of basic child needs ("We are unable to give a rating due to neglect of children," "children's not games," "night time not There in children"). At Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam,ALLAGADDA (RPRP), a parent stated: "If the caretaker calls at all, there is no reply," indicating a breakdown in caretaker-parent accountability. At Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam,THIMMAPUR, parents noted the absence of a permanent caretaker, forcing a teacher to double up — "which is detrimental" to the children. One emotionally charged complaint from Eluru's Dwarka Tirumala campus noted: "Chalasamsamruthi caretakarga two years chalabaga ma ammayi chusukunaru," underscoring a prolonged two-year failure.

**Affected Districts:**
TIRUPATI, ANANTAPUR, KURNOOL, NANDYAL, PRAKASAM, ELURU, SRI SATHYA SAI

**Affected Schools:**
TIRUPATI: Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam/JC,VAKADU (308 | 2); ANANTAPUR: Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam/JC,GOOTY (DPIP) (302 | 1), Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam/JC,KURUGUNTA (561 | 1), Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam,THIMMAPUR (262 | 1); KURNOOL: Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam Jr. College,CHINNATEKURU (85 | 1), Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam/JC,KAMBALAPADU (312 | 1); NANDYAL: Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam/JC,ALLAGADDA (RPRP) (113 | 1); PRAKASAM: Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam/JC,DUPADU (71 | 1); ELURU: Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam/JC,POLASANIPALLI (407 | 1); SRI SATHYA SAI: Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam/JC,MALUGURU (46 | 1)

**Suggested Action:**
District administrators must verify caretaker staffing levels at every residential school and establish minimum ratio norms (e.g., one caretaker per 30 students) with mandatory appointment within 15 days of any vacancy. A formal caretaker code of conduct — including prohibitions on verbal abuse, defined response-time obligations to parents, and structured child-monitoring protocols — should be introduced with signed acknowledgment and disciplinary accountability.

**Challenge Title:** Teacher Communication Gaps and Instructional Neglect

**Description:**
Parents report a significant breakdown in communication between teachers and families, alongside concerns about teachers failing in their core instructional duties. Key complaints include: "samacharam andlattlesu" (no information/communication), "Subject teacher matho matladatam ledu" (no conversation with subject teachers), "They are not giving information," and "We don't know if there is any progress in the children." At Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam/JC,DAKKILI, a distressed parent wrote: "Teachers is not working and staff chala thakuvani thelisindhi. konni subjects ki assalu Teachers leru antaa" — indicating that subject teachers are entirely absent. The ALLAGADDA (RPRP) compounds this grievance: "The teachers have not even called to say that this is the situation," suggesting zero proactive parent engagement. Some complaints also highlight behavioral issues: "Bad teacher hs" and "Using some bad words."

**Challenge Title:** Inadequate Healthcare and Medical Support

**Description:**
Parents express concern about the lack of responsive and competent healthcare for children residing at the schools. Complaints include: "sanitary pads are not given" (a recurring welfare issue for girl students at Koilakuntla), "my daughter is suffering from throat pain and hiccups there is no person understanding their problems" (a particularly vulnerable account from Kondapuram), "Health is not good," and "Government tablet too bad" (referring to substandard medication dispensed at Dwarka Tirumala). At Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Gurukulam/JC,DUPADU, a parent linked health neglect to broader conditions: "Neatenes ledu pellalaki and monkeys problem Health carring ledu." The absence of female sanitary product provision — raised explicitly — indicates a gender-blind gap in healthcare provisioning that could have serious welfare implications.
